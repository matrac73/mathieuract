From September 2023 to September 2024, I spent my ESSEC apprenticeship year at Onepoint, a French consulting and technology firm, within its Data.AI community. I held two roles side by side: right hand to the Chief AI Officer and tech lead of Neo, the group's generative AI assistant.

## Right hand to the Chief AI Officer

In 2023 Onepoint created an AI Office, a central unit that coordinates the group's AI topics. I worked alongside the Chief AI Officer: preparing executive reports, organising AI events and presenting new developments to consultants.

- Executive reports on the group's AI activity
- AI watch shared with the 200+ Data & AI consultants
- Representing Onepoint at trade shows: Bpifrance BIG 2023, VivaTech 2024, Big Data & AI Paris 2024
- An organisation in three circles: the AI Office, pilot partners and a network of AI relays across the group's communities

## Business development: generative AI offers by function and by sector

I took part in running the Data.AI activity: business development (more than ten deals), consultant staffing, client and partner relations. Proposals relied on a generative AI offer adapted to each executive function and to a few sectors.

- A common core: "ready-to-wear" or "tailor-made" tools, and three levels of support (learn and explore, experiment, transform)
- General management and finance: automated analysis of figures, report production, KPI calculation, evolution of jobs and skills
- Marketing and customer relations: personalised experience, automation of marketing processes, support for advisers
- IT department: help with development and testing, documentation generation, run productivity, evolution of the IT operating model
- Health: filling in records by voice dictation or document import, consistency checks on entered data, document anonymisation, translation, statistical reports

## Neo, Onepoint's generative AI assistant

Neo is Onepoint's secure generative AI copilot, used by more than 2,000 consultants and deployed at more than ten clients. I was its tech lead: developing the tool, then integrating it at clients.

- A toolbox in a single interface: conversation, document base, summaries of documents, websites and audio, web search, images, contract analysis, data science, shared prompt library
- Security and control: protected data, possible deployment on the client's infrastructure, connection to its information system and documents
- Frugality: models chosen to fit each use, and hosting in France
- An offer in three steps: Neo Playground to test the assistant on your own use cases, Neo Explore to define an AI strategy through experimentation, then deployment at scale at the client
- Support around the tool: onboarding, prompt engineering training, prioritisation of use cases
- Two awards: 2023 Grand Prix of the Cas d'Or de l'IA and 2024 gold medal at the Nuit de la data et de l'IA

## Measuring reliability: contract analysis

One of Neo's tools reads a contract and flags risky clauses, the "red flags". To know whether it can be trusted, the model's answers are compared with those of a human reviewer in an evaluation grid.

- A grid of 18 questions per contract, with a binary verdict for each
- The human answer is the reference, and each question is put to the model three times to measure its stability
- Three successive versions of the tool compared, on GPT-3.5 then GPT-4
- Separate tracking of false positives, meaning cases where the tool clears a clause that the human considers risky

## AI Factory and governance: from proof of concept to scale

Once the prototype is convincing, the client's question becomes production and organisation. Onepoint answered with an offer covering governance (the AI Office) and industrialisation (the AI Factory), illustrated by its own internal organisation.

- The "demo trap": a prototype runs neither on the real use case nor in real conditions
- A way of reading use cases by type: information retrieval, rewriting, semantic analysis, classification, generation
- Risks specific to generative AI: hallucinations, vulnerabilities of LLM applications (OWASP framework), legal framework and AI Act
- Suitable tests: functional tests, accuracy tests against a ground truth, evaluation of one LLM by another LLM
- The workstreams of an AI Factory: roles and responsibilities, demand management and prioritisation, service offer, technical framework, tooling, compliance, training and change management

## Generative AI and private equity

Onepoint prepared an offer for investment funds, at the crossroads of its AI teams and its deal teams. It is a subject I met again later at Adagia Partners.

- The augmented investor: contract review, querying a document base in natural language, target identification
- AI in due diligence: a "tech dive" to audit an AI technology (data, architecture, algorithm, tests, team), a "business dive" to assess its impact on the business plan
- A portfolio map: generative AI impact score per portfolio company, between threatened or protected companies, agile or not
- A proposed first step: a scoping phase to identify use cases, prioritise them by value and feasibility, then set a roadmap

## Responsible AI

I helped formalise Onepoint's environmental strategy for AI and its positions on responsible AI, in light of the AI Act. These positions show up in the "trusted AI" offer and in the design of Neo.

- The AI Act explained simply: obligations that depend on the risk level of each use case, and a gradual application timeline
- An inventory of AI uses: census by department, classification under the AI Act, risk matrix, recommendation plan
- Trusted AI "by design": common requirements, project method, governance, code of conduct
- Frugality as a principle: the leanest model that is sufficient for the use, and a carbon impact calculator for AI among the group's tools
