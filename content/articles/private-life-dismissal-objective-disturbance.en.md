## What happened

On 21 October 2026, the employment chamber of the Cour de cassation, France's highest civil court, rejected the appeal of a real estate branch director who had been dismissed in November 2021. He was in a relationship with an employee of his branch, the head of litigation, and had told her he was separated from his wife. His wife worked in the same branch, where she ran the co-ownership department. When the truth came out, in early October 2021, all three went on sick leave at the same time. The employer dismissed the director, not for misconduct, but for the disturbance caused in a branch of about forty people. The legal press commented on the ruling in the following days, and the LégiSocial article of 9 November is my starting point.

## What the course says: private life is a forbidden ground, with exceptions

My law course first sets out the general rule. An employer may end a permanent contract on personal grounds, provided those grounds amount to a "real and serious cause". The course gives three criteria: the cause really exists in the facts, it is precise and verifiable, and it is important enough to justify ending the contract.

The course then lists the forbidden grounds. They include discrimination, health, maternity, and any "fact belonging to private life". That last line comes with a bracket: unless the fact causes an objective disturbance in the company, or amounts to a breach of the duty of loyalty towards the employer.

These two exceptions are different in kind. The course separates disciplinary grounds, which require a fault by the employee, from non-disciplinary grounds, where nothing is held against the employee but the situation prevents the contract from continuing. A breach of a contractual duty belongs to the first kind. Objective disturbance belongs to the second: the conduct is not judged, its effects on the company are measured.

```figure
steps:
- The starting point is a fact that belongs to the employee's private life. Here, a romantic relationship.
- The principle is clear. Such a fact cannot justify a dismissal.
- Two exceptions exist. Either the fact creates an objective disturbance in the company, or it is a breach of a duty under the employment contract.
- The employer chose the first route and alleged no fault. The Cour de cassation finds the disturbance established and the dismissal based on a real and serious cause.
```

## What the notion explains about the ruling

The key word is "objective". The judge does not ask whether the director's conduct is morally acceptable. He looks for observable effects on how the company runs, which matches the "precise and verifiable" criterion of the course.

The employee made two arguments. First, that only the three people involved had reacted, whereas reactions from third parties would be needed to speak of a disturbance in the company. Second, that the sick leaves had been short. The dates are in the ruling: his from 6 to 25 October 2021, the head of litigation's from 6 to 15 October, his wife's for five days.

```figure
file: fig-2.en.svg
caption: The sick leaves of October 2021, as reported in the ruling
scroll: The three absence bars appear on the calendar, then an orange frame surrounds the period when both department heads are missing together.
steps:
- During the night of 5 to 6 October 2021, the situation breaks open. The calendar of the month serves as a reference.
- Three sick leaves begin. The director is absent for 20 days, the head of litigation for 10 days, the head of co-ownership for 5 days. The ruling gives the length of the last one and calls it simultaneous, so I draw it from the same start.
- For several days, two department heads of the same branch are absent at the same time, on top of the director.
- The employee argued that these absences were short. The Court answers that the simultaneous leave, "even of short duration", is one of the elements of the disturbance.
```

The Court relies on three elements. The first is this simultaneous absence of two department heads. The second is the fear, felt by both employees, of sanctions or consequences for their careers, since the man involved was their superior. The third is the damage to his authority over the branch's staff. None of these elements describes the relationship itself. All of them describe the branch. And all of them depend on his position: the same behaviour, from an employee with no managerial power, would not have produced the last two.

The course's distinction also explains a procedural detail. The employee complained that the judges had reasoned as if he had committed a fault. The Court replies that the court of appeal found no breach of the contract: the case did stay on the non-disciplinary branch.

## What to take away

The stakes can be put in numbers with the scale covered in the course. When a dismissal is found to lack a real and serious cause, the judge awards damages within set limits: for 5 years of service in a company of more than 11 employees, between 3 and 6 months of gross salary. When the dismissal is void, because it violates a fundamental freedom, the cap disappears and the minimum is 6 months. The commentary by the Force Ouvrière union recalls that a dismissal based on private life is void. Here the cause is found real and serious: none of this is owed.

```figure
file: fig-3.en.svg
caption: Damages awarded by the judge depending on the outcome (course example, 5 years of service)
scroll: The three possible outcomes stack up on a scale in months of salary, the last one in orange at zero.
steps:
- If the dismissal is found to lack a real and serious cause, the scale applies. For 5 years of service, the judge awards between 3 and 6 months of gross salary.
- If the dismissal is void, the scale no longer applies. The minimum is 6 months and there is no cap.
- If the cause is real and serious, as it is here, the judge awards none of these damages.
```

This does not mean the employee leaves with nothing. A non-disciplinary dismissal gives a right to statutory severance pay: a quarter of a month's salary per year up to 10 years, a third beyond. For a hiring in August 2010 and a dismissal in November 2021, about 11 years and 3 months, the course formula gives 10 × 1/4 + 1.25 × 1/3 ≈ 2.9 months of salary. This is my own calculation, not a figure from the ruling, which does not say what the employee received.

The notion also has limits. It sets no threshold: no number of days of absence, no number of people affected. The Court writes that the court of appeal "could deduce" the disturbance from its findings, which leaves the assessment to the lower courts, case by case. And the ruling does not say that a relationship between colleagues is a ground for dismissal. Private life remains protected; what was judged is the state of the branch.
